Mars Combustion Physics

Can a Candle Burn in Open Martian Air?
🚫 IMPOSSIBLE IN AMBIENT AIR
⚠️
Natural Open Flame is Physically Impossible
A candle cannot ignite or burn in the open Martian atmosphere. If exposed, any flame is immediately and violently extinguished by three simultaneous physical mechanisms:
1. Severe Hypoxia (0.13% O₂) Limiting Oxygen Index (LOI) Collapse
LOI Deficit

Paraffin wax (C₂₅H₅₂) requires a minimum LOI of ≥ 14.5%–15.0% O₂ to sustain combustion. Mars has only 0.13% O₂ (partial pressure ~0.8 Pa vs Earth's 21,300 Pa). Radical chain branching (H + O₂ → OH + O) instantly halts.

2. Near-Vacuum (0.636 kPa / 0.09 psi) Sub-Armstrong Flash Evaporation
0.006 atm

At <1% of Earth's atmospheric pressure, volatile wax hydrocarbons flash-boil and disperse rapidly into the ambient vacuum before a combustible fuel-air diffusion zone can form around the wick.

3. Active CO₂ Quenching (95.3% CO₂) Chemical & Thermal Fire Suppression
95.3% CO₂

Dense carbon dioxide possesses high molar heat capacity, acting as an active chemical and thermal fire-extinguishing blanket that siphons reaction enthalpy and deactivates flame radicals.

🔬 Why Is This Candle Burning In The Scene?

The candle is housed inside a hermetically sealed quartz test chamber (NASA CIR-M Rig) supplied with artificial habitat atmosphere (21% O₂, 101.3 kPa) piped through the umbilical from the Ice Home.

This isolates and tests only Mars's 0.38g reduced gravity: natural buoyant convection drops by 62% (Grashof Number Gr ≈ 38% of Earth), transforming the tall teardrop flame into a wide, spherical-dome envelope with lower soot production.
