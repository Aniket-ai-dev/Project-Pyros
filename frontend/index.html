1. Navier-Stokes Buoyancy & Convection

In normal Earth gravity, flames are shaped by buoyant natural convection. Thermal expansion decreases the gas density in the flame zone, creating an upward buoyant force described by the Boussinesq approximation:

ρ (∂u/∂t + u · ∇u) = -∇p + μ ∇²u + ρ₀ β (T - T₀) g

The buoyant upward acceleration draws surrounding air inward and upward, stretching the flame into its familiar teardrop shape and driving convective flickering at 10–14 Hz.

2. The ISS Microgravity Spherical Diffusion Regime (g = 0.0)

In orbital freefall aboard the International Space Station (ISS), the effective gravitational acceleration is zero (g = 0.0 m/s²). The buoyant term drops out entirely.

Gr → 0 ⟹ ∂Y/∂t = D ∇²Y - ω̇

Combustion becomes purely governed by isotropic molecular diffusion. Oxygen diffuses symmetrically from all radial directions toward the wick, and combustion products diffuse outward. Because there is no buoyant draft to stretch the gas, the flame naturally settles into a calm, ethereal spherical blue diffusion dome.

3. The 4 Anatomical Zones of a Wax Candle Flame

A real candle flame consists of four distinct physical zones established by diffusion kinetics:

  • Zone 1: Sapphire-Blue Radical Base Cup: Hugs the lower 20% of the wick. Rich in excited CH* (431 nm) and C₂* (516 nm) radicals formed where incoming fresh ambient oxygen first meets vaporized wax.
  • Zone 2: Dark Non-Luminous Pyrolysis Core: Located directly above the black wick. Solid paraffin is vaporized into hydrocarbon gas (C₂₀H₄₂ to C₃₀H₆₂). Oxygen has not penetrated here, and temperature is too low for soot incandescence.
  • Zone 3: Luminous Golden-White Incandescent Core: Carbon nanoparticles (soot) form and are heated to 1,600 K–1,850 K, glowing brilliantly via Planck blackbody radiation.
  • Zone 4: Warm Amber Mantle & Tip: The outer envelope where soot particles meet sufficient oxygen and are fully combusted into CO₂ and H₂O.
4. Dimensionless Numbers Reference
Grashof Number (Gr = g β ΔT L³ / ν²): Measures the ratio of buoyant forces to viscous friction. On Earth, Gr ≈ 10⁵ (strong convection); on the ISS, Gr = 0 (zero convection).
Rayleigh Number (Ra = Gr · Pr): Governs the transition to free natural convection. Below the critical threshold (Ra < 10³), convection is suppressed and diffusion dominates.
Froude Number (Fr = u / √(gL)): Compares inertial momentum from injection velocity to gravitational buoyancy forces.
Péclet Number (Pe = u L / α): Ratio of advective transport rate to thermal/mass diffusivity.
