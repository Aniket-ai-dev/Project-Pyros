From Spacecraft Smoke Detection to Lunar Regolith Dust: The SAME ➔ DAFT Evolution

During the Apollo lunar expeditions, abrasive lunar regolith tracked inside the cabin caused severe respiratory inflammation and degraded environmental life-support systems. In microgravity fire research, the optical and ionization sensors developed for the SAME (Smoke Aerosol Measurement Experiment) and SAME-R studies were discovered to be exquisitely sensitive to microscopic particle distributions.

NASA engineers directly repurposed this identical flight hardware for the DAFT (Dust and Aerosol Measurement Feasibility Test) and DAFT-2 investigations to measure cabin dust aerosols. For future Artemis Moon bases and pressurized rovers, this provides the critical design precedent for dual-mode aerosol monitoring: distinguishing benign airborne lunar regolith from deadly electrical or pyrolytic smoke particles to eliminate false alarms.
